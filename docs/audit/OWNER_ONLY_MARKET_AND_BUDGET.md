## A1. What the market looks like for this stack

GHOST needs five kinds of expertise in one engagement: applied cryptography and protocol analysis (Olm, X-Wing/ML-KEM composition, MLS, RFC 9474 blind signatures), Rust code review, Android application security, Tor/onion-service knowledge, and Monero integration (view-only wallet, payouts). No firm in the table below has published work on all five. The public record points to a **crypto-protocol specialist plus an application-security firm**, either one prime with subcontractors or two parallel engagements (A = cryptography/protocols; B, C, D = application, services, supply chain). Firms' public reports differ in how much of the engagement is stated: the "shape" column gives what the report itself states.

**Reading the result:**

1. **The plan's "6-8 weeks" is a calendar figure that nobody sized.** At the plan's scope (S2) it requires 8-16 auditors in parallel for 8 weeks; the public engagements above ran with 2-6 (SRLabs OpenMLS: 4 for 12 weeks; Cure53: 6 for 2 weeks to 56 days; Least Authority: 5). **Plan for 10-14 weeks with 6-8 auditors, or split the tiers across two firms, or accept S1** (inferred). This figure is for the owner only: the RFQ text does not state it (see A4).
2. **Test code is not review effort.** There are 74,430 lines of Kotlin unit tests, 45,997 lines of Rust integration tests and 13,950 lines of Rust test-only code inside `src/` (together about 1.8 times the production code); the table does not charge for them, assuming the auditor judges the oracles (T1, T2, T32) by sampling and by the mutants.
3. **The uncertainty is at least +-50 %** on each line (two or three data points per band, unmeasured sizes of the comparison code, unknown full-time ratios). Ask each bidder to price **per tier and per sub-tier** (RFQ section B8) and compare their person-days to this table: a bid far below it for tier A1 should be questioned, not welcomed.
4. **Price** = person-days x day rate x risk/rush margin. The OSTIF-derived USD 1,200/day would put the plan scope at roughly USD 0.4 - 0.8 million (312-654 person-days), S1 at roughly USD 0.2 - 0.4 million (154-335) and S0 at roughly USD 0.35 - 0.47 million (288-389) **before** commercial margins. Treat these as **at least** figures for the order of magnitude, not as an estimate of any firm's rate; commercial closed-source rates are probably higher (inferred, weak).

**Two-stage option (inferred):** a pre-audit review of A1 (the DM composition, ranks 1 and 3 of the priority list) and C can start before Phases 9-14 finish, because the Rust crates are stable in structure; the exit gate of the master plan (row 15) is then closed by a smaller second stage covering what changed (MLS, UI, media, distribution) and the retest. This lowers the schedule risk of one 6-8-week block and finds design-level problems before more code depends on them. The cost is two kick-offs and a rework risk if the DM crate changes materially after stage 1.

## A3. Decisions only the owner can take (also in `README-RO.md`)

1. **Which tiers** (A, B, C, D, options A2 MLS, A5 threat-model review, B6 UI) and **which scenario** (S0, S1, S2) to request; whether to **split** between a cryptography firm and an application-security firm.
2. **Publication policy:** ADR-38 point 1 says audit reports are published in full, with retests, before the first public distribution. The RFQ asks for that by default (B5, B6) and for a **public-summary-only** alternative to be priced; choosing the alternative contradicts ADR-38 and its "rejected alternative" note (a summary alone is insufficient; the Threema analysis at USENIX Security 2023 is cited there). Decide before sending.
3. **Source access terms:** NDA and limited-use licence text (the root `LICENSE` grants nothing); whether the auditor gets full git history or a snapshot; no GitHub collaborator access (`BUILD_AND_REVIEW_GUIDE.md` section 1.2).
4. **Scope freeze date** and which of Phases 9-14 must be closed or explicitly waived before kick-off (`AUDIT_SCOPE.md` section 0).
5. The open decisions of `KNOWN_ISSUES.md` section 2 and 4 that change code under audit (decide before the freeze, not during the audit).
6. Budget ceiling and whether the retest is included in a fixed price.
7. Whether to commission **English versions** of THREAT_MODEL, INVARIANTS and LIMITE (192 KB of Romanian text) before the audit.
8. **The rule for known issues** in the RFQ's severity annex (B11): the default written there is that findings the project already knows still count towards "0 open Critical or High" when the auditor rates them so (the other options: exclude the listed items, or count only items the owner has not accepted in writing). Two exit-gate claims that the annex floors at High (T2 and T32) are red in the project's own nightlies today.
9. **Whether the RFQ states a calendar length and team size.** The 2026-10-03 text said "we do not disclose our person-day estimate" and in the same sentence gave "10-14 weeks with six to eight auditors", which is that estimate in other units and anchors the bids. The revision keeps the non-disclosure and drops the figure; reinstate it only if you prefer to anchor.
10. **The pre-bid NDA step** added to the RFQ timeline (B0): bidders sign a one-way NDA before they receive the audit package, and bid from the package. Without it, bids are priced from the RFQ text alone.
11. **Commit identities** (the full-history bundle exposes the owner's personal e-mail addresses: `KNOWN_ISSUES.md` KI-SUP-9) and whether to hand over a snapshot without history instead.
12. **CI evidence at the freeze**: export the logs and gate reports (logs expire after 90 days, artifacts after 3) and whether to raise the retention (`KNOWN_ISSUES.md` KI-SUP-8).
13. **The `dm-fuzz` `bundle` target**: correct its property (one line, `EVIDENCE_INDEX.md` section 6) before the freeze, so that the nightly gives a signal again (`KNOWN_ISSUES.md` KI-DM-F). The package does not change code.
